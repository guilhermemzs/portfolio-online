Generative AI in engineering: from plausible answers to verifiable information

Using generative AI in engineering requires independent checks, traceable sources and a clear account of who reviews a decision.

By Guilherme Menezes Pereira · Editorial date 4 October 2026

Abridged English edition · Portuguese original published 6 November 2026

When fluency conceals uncertainty

A confident explanation may contain a missing unit, a fabricated reference or an unsuitable assumption. The quality of the prose tells us little about the quality of the engineering evidence. Decide how an answer will be checked before using it.

Match the review to the task

Brainstorming a seminar topic, summarising a manual and evaluating structural safety have different consequences if an answer is wrong. NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework describes governance, mapping, measurement and management. It offers a way to organise review responsibilities.

Check what each source supports

Verify the author, title and passage, then read its scope. A study reporting that a waste material improved concrete strength applies to particular preparation methods, proportions, test ages and reference mixes. Those details determine what the result can support.

Read supplied documents critically

Giving a model the source files still leaves the question of whether it found and interpreted the relevant passage. In the experiments reported in Lost in the Middle, performance varied with the position of information in context. Those results describe the tested conditions.

Recalculate independently

For a hypothetical rectangular area measuring 8 m by 5 m with a uniform thickness of 0.12 m, the geometric volume is 8 × 5 × 0.12 = 4.8 m³. An answer of 480 m³ fails an order-of-magnitude check. An answer of 4.8 m² has the wrong unit. Any allowance for losses needs a separate assumption. This example illustrates verification and supplies no structural design or complete purchasing estimate.

Keep a traceable record

For a synthesis of three inspection reports, record which page supports each statement. Distinguish observations from inferences and identify incompatible criteria. When one report changes, the record shows which conclusions require review.

Protect data and independent judgement

Check which material the chosen tool may receive and whether sharing it is authorised. A fictional example may serve the study task. Try explaining a conclusion, reproducing a calculation and identifying conditions that would change the answer.

Build checks into everyday work

Describe the intended use, ask for documented facts and assumptions separately, review consequential claims and record unresolved questions. Keep the evidence and the person responsible for the final decision identifiable.
