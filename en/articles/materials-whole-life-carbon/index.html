Sustainable construction: comparing materials by whole-life carbon

Comparing construction materials requires equivalent performance, clear assessment boundaries and traceable emissions data.

By Guilherme Menezes Pereira · Editorial date 6 September 2026

Abridged English edition · Portuguese original published 6 November 2026

In this article
  1. Ask the question before buying
  2. Define the life-cycle boundary
  3. Compare equivalent functions
  4. Work through the numbers
  5. Read environmental declarations carefully
  6. Turn the comparison into a design decision
  7. Make the reasoning reviewable
  8. References

Ask the question before buying

Start with the function the design must provide. Reusing a structure or reorganising an existing space may change how much new material is needed. The numerical examples below are teaching examples and do not represent suppliers, test results or one of my own construction projects.

Define the life-cycle boundary

Life-cycle assessment defines a goal and scope, inventories inputs and outputs, evaluates impacts and interprets results. For climate impacts, emissions can be expressed as carbon dioxide equivalent, or CO₂e. State which stages are included. In the cited RICS framework, A1-A3 covers the product stage, A4 transport to site and A5 construction. Later maintenance and replacement can change the comparison.

Compare equivalent functions

One kilogram of each raw material may reveal a manufacturing difference without telling a designer which wall to choose. Compare complete systems that meet the same performance requirements over the same study period. Record the quantity needed for each.

Work through the numbers

Suppose two fictional alternatives provide the same element. A uses 1,000 units at 2 kg CO₂e per unit, plus 300 kg for transport and installation, for a partial total of 2,300 kg CO₂e. B uses 800 units at 2.5 kg CO₂e per unit, plus 100 kg, for a partial total of 2,100 kg CO₂e. Both have 2,000 kg CO₂e of manufacturing emissions. B is about 8.7% lower within this boundary. A replacement during the study period could change the result.

Read environmental declarations carefully

Check the declared unit, product category rules, covered stages and whether the figures represent a specific product or a sector average. Preserve the document version and page for each value. Two published declarations can use assumptions that prevent direct comparison.

Turn the comparison into a design decision

Record quantities, sources, transport distances, waste assumptions and uncertainty. If the outcome depends heavily on a route that has yet to be confirmed, calculate distance scenarios and identify when the preferred option changes.

Make the reasoning reviewable

A reviewer should be able to reconstruct the calculation and explain which new evidence might change the recommendation. Climate impact is one consideration alongside technical performance, durability and the other environmental effects of construction.

References

  • RICS. Whole life carbon assessment for the built environment. 2ª edição, 2023; versão 3, agosto de 2024.
  • UNEP. Building Materials and the Climate: Constructing a New Future. 2023.
  • U.S. EPA. Criteria for Product Category Rules to Support the Label Program for Low Embodied Carbon Construction Materials. Versão 1, agosto de 2024.
