From plant to pavement: what quality control reveals about an asphalt mixture

An academic visit to the BR-040 asphalt plant raised questions about sampling, production, compaction and traceability.

By Guilherme Menezes Pereira · Editorial date 20 September 2026

Abridged English edition · Portuguese original published 6 November 2026

A question from a site visit

On 19 September 2026 I visited Pavidez Engenharia’s mobile asphalt plant with FUMEC’s civil engineering programme during EPR Via Mineira’s works on the BR-040. This article develops questions prompted by the visit. I did not conduct an audit, measure pavement performance or issue a technical opinion on the plant.

Understand the mixture

Asphalt concrete combines aggregate, binder and air voids. Aggregate grading, binder properties and compaction affect how the mixture behaves. The pavement also depends on its underlying layers, drainage and loading conditions.

Connect production to the mix design

The corrected version of DNIT 031/2024-ES addresses materials, equipment, execution, sampling and acceptance. Which specification applies to a project depends on its contract documents. A temperature mentioned during a visit cannot establish a universal operating rule.

Give each sample a history

Record the sample identifier, time, origin, sampling point, lot or road section, test method and person recording the result. If two hypothetical reports disagree, first establish whether they cover the same lot, use comparable sampling and apply compatible test methods. Preserve unfavourable results as part of that record.

Relate compaction to placement

Layer thickness and nominal maximum aggregate size affect the ability to achieve the required density. The 2019 National Academies synthesis examines that relationship. Consider haulage, waiting time, placement and rolling together. A roller-pass count taken out of context cannot prescribe quality.

Distinguish production control from acceptance

Contractor quality control helps adjust production. Agency acceptance evaluates compliance with the applicable criteria. Independent assurance examines aspects of the measurement system. A report is easier to interpret when the result is linked to its sample, the sample to a lot or section, and the decision to the relevant criterion.

Turn observations into study questions

Before a visit, sketch the production sequence and prepare a few precise questions. During the visit, follow the team's guidance and distinguish direct observations from explanations given by staff. A photograph of a specimen establishes neither its test result nor the quality of a finished road.

Follow the links between stages

Studying one test in depth can reveal how materials, statistics and site decisions fit together. For me, the value of the BR-040 visit lies in asking better questions and recognising what the available observations can support.
