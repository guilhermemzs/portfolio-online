From process to software: building reliable tools for everyday work

Reliable internal software starts with the process, the people who make decisions and the evidence that a task was completed.

By Guilherme Menezes Pereira · Editorial date 27 September 2026

Abridged English edition · Portuguese original published 6 November 2026

Find the decision behind the screen

A lesson booking combines a teacher, a class, a time and availability. An inspection combines people, equipment, an address and access. Before designing an interface, identify which decision is difficult, which error keeps recurring and who needs the result.

Follow one request from start to finish

In a hypothetical make-up lesson request, staff check availability, agree on a time, record the lesson and notify the teacher. Sending confirmation before the booking is recorded can promise a slot that was never reserved. Mapping the transitions reveals where validation belongs.

Define a requirement you can test

Specify what the system should do when a teacher is already booked, what the user should see and who can resolve the conflict. Write a valid example, an invalid example and an acceptance criterion. Observe the real workflow to verify that the rule serves its purpose.

Separate records from events

A teacher needs a stable identifier even if their name changes. A recurring timetable supplies a pattern, while a particular lesson records a cancellation or change on a specific date. In engineering, a similar distinction separates a piece of equipment from inspections performed on it.

Test awkward cases

Test missing fields, reversed intervals, interrupted requests and two people reserving the same resource simultaneously. A hypothetical 14:30-15:30 booking overlaps a 14:00-15:00 lesson. Whether a 15:00-16:00 booking is permitted depends on the agreed rule. Enforce essential constraints where the data is written.

Design privacy and access into the workflow

List the information each role needs, the purpose of collection and who may change a record. Keep demonstration data separate from real records. Test whether an authorised person can restore and understand a backup.

Help people understand the outcome

Use familiar terms such as “Confirm booking” and give errors a practical next step. Keyboard controls and labelled fields matter in internal tools too. Trial a limited workflow, observe where people abandon it and use those observations to improve it.

Evaluate changes honestly

Time to confirmation, conflicts caught before confirmation and corrections are possible indicators. Record workload, staffing and the observation period before attributing a change to the software. This portfolio presents the work without claiming unmeasured gains.

Connect decisions to implementation

Useful systems make responsibilities visible and let someone verify what happened. Education, management and engineering all benefit when a tool grows from a clearly understood task.
