Planning under uncertainty: the critical path, constraints and reliable commitments

A reliable schedule connects dependencies and uncertainty with the resources and decisions required to start work.

By Guilherme Menezes Pereira · Editorial date 11 October 2026

Abridged English edition · Portuguese original published 6 November 2026

In this article
  1. Model work that can actually happen
  2. Identify deliverables and dependencies
  3. Calculate the critical path
  4. Account for uncertainty
  5. Assign each constraint an owner
  6. Measure promises kept
  7. Investigate missed commitments
  8. Allow for competing demands
  9. Learn from actual conditions
  10. References

Model work that can actually happen

A calendar may show a Monday start even though materials have yet to arrive or the team is assigned elsewhere. A workable schedule connects dates to deliverables, dependencies, available people and site conditions.

Identify deliverables and dependencies

Define how someone will recognise completion. “Deliver the reviewed methods section with checked references” gives a team a clearer target than “advance the report”. Draw the dependency network and examine activities that compete for the same resource.

Calculate the critical path

In a hypothetical example with sufficient resources, A takes two working days. B takes three days after A, and C takes four days after A. D takes two days and must wait for both. A–B–D totals seven days and A–C–D totals eight. B has one day of total float. A two-day delay to C moves completion to day ten. If only B grows from three days to five, the critical path changes and the project takes nine days.

Account for uncertainty

A four-day estimate might describe normal conditions or an optimistic assumption. Record the basis for each estimate and consider favourable, likely and adverse scenarios. Risks can affect several activities together, as persistent rain does on outdoor work.

Assign each constraint an owner

Before committing to next week's activity, ask who will release the area, confirm materials and arrange equipment. Specify a deadline and the evidence that will show the constraint is resolved. This look-ahead work is central to the Last Planner System.

Measure promises kept

Percent Plan Complete compares completed commitments with planned commitments for a period. In a hypothetical week with ten agreed activities and seven finished to the agreed criteria, PPC is 7 ÷ 10 × 100 = 70%. This measures those commitments and cannot establish physical project completion or labour productivity.

Investigate missed commitments

Keep the original baseline when updating a forecast. Record the immediate cause of each missed commitment and investigate the decision or condition behind it. A missing delivery may point to late purchasing, incomplete specifications or an unrealistic lead time.

Allow for competing demands

A person who handles urgent requests has less predictable time for planned tasks. Make that demand visible, limit concurrent work and prepare alternatives in case a task is blocked.

Learn from actual conditions

Compare the agreed sequence and durations with what took place. A small, documented project can teach more about dependencies and risk than a detailed schedule whose assumptions nobody can explain.
